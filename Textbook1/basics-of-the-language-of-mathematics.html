    Section 1.1 Basics of the Language of Mathematics

    Throughout history, mathematical ideas and methods have been used. Describing these ideas and methods using natural language can be very lengthy or very difficult. Over time, symbols, characters, words, definitions, and so forth, were developed to be used in mathematics. Hence there is a language of mathematics separate from natural language, but also intertwined with natural language. At least two things we want to address are the confusion caused by the intertwining with natural language and how a person is expected to learn mathematics. On the subject of how a person is expected to learn mathematics, maybe they are expected learn merely by observing and repeating as a child would begin to learn natural language. We will take the direction that this is not enough and attempt to give more explanation for some basics of the language of mathematics.
    🔗

    Subsection 1.1.1 Basics of Expressions: Terminology, Construction and Order for expressions, and Entanglement with Natural Language

    What is a mathematical expression? One way to look at an expression is to think of it as a set of instructions to perform a task. A mathematical expression would be a clause as part of a sentence. To construct an expression we use symbols, characters, letters, and so forth. These objects can represent constants, variables, operations, and order of performing tasks. Some of these objects come from natural language, but are used differently. We will need to be careful and note when an object is used differently in mathematics from the way it is used in natural language.
    🔗

    Subsubsection 1.1.1.1 Types of Numbers

    Numbers come in many forms. Some of the numbers we use look like \(3\text{,}\) \(3.465\text{,}\) \(\frac{3}{5}\text{,}\) \(\sqrt{5}\text{.}\) Numbers are classified into different types. The natural numbers are those used for counting \(1,2,3,4,5,6,\) …. The whole numbers are the natural numbers with \(0\) attached. The integers are the whole numbers and all their negatives. The rational numbers are all possible fractions (ratios) of integers. It turns out there are numbers that cannot be written as ratios of integers, including those numbers makes up what are called the real numbers.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsubsection 1.1.1.2 Constants, Variables, Operations

    Constants are known objects that do not change. Some constants are familiar to most people as numbers such as 12.35. Other constants appear using a natural language letter such as \(e\) or \(\pi\text{,}\) causing us to decide if the letter is mathematics or natural language. This is usually determined by the context where it is used.
    🔗
    Variables are objects that could change. Variables are usually represented by natural language letters such as \(x\text{.}\) Here again we have to decide if a letter is a math symbol or natural language. Variables are sometimes used to represent unknowns. An unknown in many cases is a constant, but we do not know what their value is. We use a variable for the unknown to try different values in hopes of finding the true value.
    🔗
    An operation is a task to perform a process on one or more values to produce a value. Addition of two numbers is an operation, the symbol for this operation is \(+\text{.}\) There are many operations such as multiplication, square root, square, and absolute value. Many of these operations have familiar symbols used in expressions.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsubsection 1.1.1.3 Building Basic Expressions

    Given the expression \(3x-2(y-1)\text{,}\) it could be read out as “3 followed by x subtract 2 followed by open parentheses y subtract 1 close parentheses”. Notice that we read out math expressions in natural language sometimes with math words included. What does the expression mean in the language of mathematics? In natural language there is an order that a clause is to be read, there is a way to group letters into words, and so forth. For mathematical expressions there are also rules for the order of processing and grouping. One convention is the interpretation of a whole or decimal number followed directly by some other type of object; this indicates multiplication. Another convention is to process from left to right, but this convention is overridden by the rules for order of operation. There are many operations the basic ones being \(+\text{,}\) \(-\text{,}\) \(\times\text{,}\) \(\div\text{,}\) powers, and roots. These are to be processed in a particular order; powers and roots first, followed by multiplication and division, followed by addition and subtraction. The order of operation can be changed with grouping symbols such as (), {}, [], \(\sqrt{}\text{.}\) Expressions inside grouping symbols are processed first. For the expression \(3x-2(y-1)\text{,}\) it should be processed as y minus 1, that quantity times 2 then that result subtracted from the quantity of 3 times x.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsubsection 1.1.1.4 Terms and Factors

    We must take notice of the facts that subtract is a type of addition and division is a type of multiplication. For example subtracting two is adding negative two and dividing by 2 is multiplying by \(\frac{1}{2}\text{.}\) For discussions that follow, many statements about addition apply to subtract and many statements about multiplication apply to division. We want to consider terms and factors in expressions. The words term and factor in mathematics do not have the same definitions as they do for people using natural language. A further complication is that the word term has different definitions in mathematics depending on context. Here we will consider terms and factors in the context of expressions. If the last operations to be performed in expression are additions (subtractions) then the parts separated by \(+\) and \(-\) are called terms. If the last operations to be performed is multiplication (division) the parts separated by multiplication (division) are called factors.
    🔗
    Lets look at three example expressions:
    • \(6x^{2}+7x-5\text{,}\) this expression presented with three terms, although there are some multiplications they are not the last operation.
      🔗
      🔗
    • \((2x-1)(3x+5)\text{,}\) this expression is presented with two factors, although there are some additions they are not the last operation.
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(x(6x+7)-5\text{,}\) this expression is presented with two terms, although there are other additions they are not part of the last operation.
      🔗
      🔗
    The terms of the expression \(6x^{2}+7x-5\) have no additions and the expression is said to be expanded or multiplied out. The expression \((2x-1)(3x+5)\) is said to be factored. The expression \(x(6x+7)-5\) is neither factored nor expanded.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsubsection 1.1.1.5 Equivalent Expressions

    In algebra a student may ask “Why are we doing this?”. One of the reasons we consider things like factoring in algebra is to rephrase things to make them more understandable or easier to use. This is done in natural language; A statement is made in natural language and then is modified to make is clearer to the audience or to make it more interesting as in poetry. Hence, we are concerned with when two expressions say the same thing, that is, when two expressions are equivalent. In mathematical terms, two expressions are equivalent if substitution for the variables in the expressions produced the same result.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.1.2 Equations, Inequalities, Solutions

    What are equations and inequalities? Basically, equations and inequalities are constructed by comparing two objects using phrases such as “is equal to”, “is less than”, and so forth. One example in the language of mathematics is \(2x^{3}-7y=2x+3\text{.}\) This example takes two expressions and compares them by “is equal to”. Notice that the expressions in the example, \(2x^{3}-7y\) and \(2x+3\) are just phases in natural language but the equation \(2x^{3}-7y=2x+3\) is a statement in natural language, that is, a type of complete sentence. An example of an inequality is \(3y+x\leq5y^{2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    When we present an equation or inequality, we are making a statement. The statement could be true or false. If an equation or inequality contains variables, the is true or false depending on the values substituted for the variables. If a substitution for the variables yields a true statement then it is a solution for the equation or inequality. Another word for an equation solution is root. The word root is also used for things like “the cube root of 4”, in notation \(\sqrt[3]{4}\text{,}\) but this is coming from the same meaning, that is, \(\sqrt[3]{4}\) is a solution for \(x^{3}=4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    There is another setting for solutions where solutions are called zeros. If an equation is presented with \(0\) on one side of the \(=\text{,}\) such as \(x^{2}+5x+6=0\text{,}\) then the solutions can be called zeros. Additionally, they are called zeros of the expression, for the example, zeros of \(x^{2}+5x+6\text{.}\)
